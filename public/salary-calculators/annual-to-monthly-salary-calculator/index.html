Gross pay conversion guide

How an annual to monthly salary calculator converts pay between every period

This calculator takes a rate of pay at one frequency and shows it at all the others: annual, monthly, biweekly, weekly, daily and hourly. Enter a $60,000 annual salary and it returns $5,000 a month; enter a $30 hourly rate and it builds the annual figure from your hours and weeks first. It exists because pay is quoted in whatever unit suits the person quoting it, and comparing two offers, or checking a payslip against a contract, means getting both onto the same footing.

Every figure is gross — before income tax, payroll tax, pension and other deductions. Gross is the right basis for comparing offers, because tax depends on where you live and your wider circumstances. For the after-tax number, use the Salary Calculator.

How to Guide

What this calculator does

It normalises your input to an annual amount, then divides that back out into each pay period. Monthly is annual divided by 12; biweekly is annual divided by 26; weekly is annual divided by your paid weeks per year; daily and hourly come from the weekly figure and your workdays and hours per week. Nothing is chained through intermediate rounding — every period is derived directly from the annual figure.

How to use this calculator

  1. Enter the pay amount you already know.
  2. Set the frequency that amount is paid at — annual, monthly, biweekly, weekly, daily or hourly.
  3. Adjust hours per week, weeks per year and workdays per week if your schedule is not the standard 40 / 52 / 5.
  4. Select Calculate. The monthly figure is highlighted; the rest are in the results panel.
  5. Read the monthly figure for budgeting and the annual figure for comparing offers.

How to interpret the results

If a figure looks off, check the schedule inputs first. A part-time contract at 30 hours a week or a term-time role at 39 paid weeks produces very different weekly and hourly numbers from the 40 / 52 default.

Worked example

A $60,000 annual salary is $5,000 a month, $2,307.69 biweekly, and $1,153.85 a week on a 52-week year. At 40 hours a week that is about $28.85 an hour. Start instead from $30 an hour at 37.5 hours over 46 paid weeks and the annual figure is $51,750 — a reminder that an hourly rate only becomes a salary once the hours and weeks are pinned down.

Why monthly is not four weekly paychecks

A month is a little longer than four weeks, so annual ÷ 12 is larger than four times annual ÷ 52. If you are paid every four weeks you get 13 paychecks a year, not 12, and the right figure is annual ÷ 13. Treating four-weekly pay as if it were monthly is a common mistake that never quite reconciles against a real payslip.

Which pay-period figure should you budget from?

Budget from the period you are actually paid in. If your salary lands monthly, use the monthly figure and hold a small buffer for months with an extra bill. If you are paid weekly or biweekly, budget from that figure and remember that two months a year contain a third biweekly paycheck — useful months to move money into savings.

Part-time and non-standard schedules

The schedule inputs are what make this accurate for anything other than a full-time year. Reduce hours per week for part-time work, reduce weeks per year for term-time or seasonal contracts, and change workdays per week for a compressed or four-day pattern. The monthly and biweekly figures do not move — they are fixed fractions of the annual salary — but the weekly, daily and hourly figures do.

Edge cases and limits

  • Zero or blank inputs: the calculator waits for a positive pay amount and a positive number of weeks before it returns anything.
  • Very low hours: allowed — a 1-hour-per-week arrangement still converts, though the annual figure will be small.
  • Semi-monthly (24 pays): not a separate option because it equals the monthly figure divided by two.
  • Overtime and bonuses: not included. This converts one steady rate; use the Overtime Pay Calculator or Bonus Calculator for variable pay.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you convert an annual salary to monthly pay?

Divide the annual salary by 12. A $60,000 salary is $5,000 a month before tax. The calculator does this and also shows biweekly, weekly, daily and hourly equivalents.

Does this show gross or take-home pay?

Gross pay only, before income tax, payroll tax and deductions. For take-home pay after tax, use the Salary Calculator.

Why is monthly pay not the same as four weekly paychecks?

Monthly pay is annual divided by 12; four weekly paychecks is annual divided by 13. Most months have a bit more than four weeks, so the two figures never quite match.

Can I start from an hourly rate or a weekly wage instead?

Yes. Set the frequency to whatever your amount is — hourly, daily, weekly, biweekly, monthly or annual — and every other period is worked out from it.

What do hours per week and weeks per year change?

Only the weekly, daily and hourly figures. Monthly and biweekly pay are fixed fractions of the annual amount and do not depend on your schedule.

How do I convert monthly pay back to an annual salary?

Multiply the monthly figure by 12. Set the frequency to Monthly, enter the amount, and the annual salary is shown in the results.

Does this include overtime or bonuses?

No. It converts one steady rate of pay between periods. Use the Overtime Pay Calculator or Bonus Calculator for variable pay.

Which biweekly figure does it use — 24 or 26 pays a year?

Biweekly means every two weeks, so 26 pays a year (annual divided by 26). A semi-monthly schedule of 24 pays a year is the same as the monthly figure divided by two.

Are my inputs stored anywhere?

No. Everything runs in your browser and nothing is sent anywhere or saved.
