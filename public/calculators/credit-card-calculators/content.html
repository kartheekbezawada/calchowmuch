Five focused tools for credit card debt: work out what your minimum payment actually costs, compare a fixed repayment plan, test whether a balance transfer is worth the fee, and choose an order to clear several cards.

Start with the question you actually have

  • Credit Card Minimum Payment Calculator — work out your minimum payment and see how many years minimum-only repayment takes. On a typical card a $5,000 balance runs close to 26 years and costs more in interest than the original balance.
  • Credit Card Payoff Calculator — set a fixed monthly payment and see the payoff date and total interest it buys you.
  • Balance Transfer Calculator — weigh the transfer fee and promo APR against the revert rate to see whether moving the balance genuinely saves money.
  • Credit Card Consolidation Calculator — compare keeping the cards against folding them into one fixed-rate loan.
  • Debt Payoff Calculator — order several balances by snowball or avalanche and compare what each approach costs.

How credit card repayment works

A credit card minimum payment is normally the greater of a flat dollar floor or a small percentage of the balance, with that month's interest and fees added on top. Because the percentage is applied to a balance that shrinks each month, the required payment shrinks too — which is what stretches minimum-only repayment across decades rather than years.

That makes the order of questions fairly consistent. Find out what minimum-only actually costs first, because it sets the baseline every other option is measured against. Then check what a fixed higher payment changes. Only then is it worth pricing a balance transfer or a consolidation loan, since both are judged against the interest you would otherwise pay.

All five calculators run entirely in your browser. Nothing you enter is sent anywhere or stored.
