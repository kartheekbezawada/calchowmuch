Birthdays move because a calendar year is not an exact number of weeks. A normal year adds one leftover day, and a leap year adds an extra day in February. That is why the same date rotates through different weekdays over time.

February 29 birthdays stay on February 29 in leap years. In non-leap preview years and non-leap next-birthday calculations, the planner uses February 28 so the answer stays practical for real-world planning.

How to work out what day of the week you were born without a calculator

If you want to check the answer by hand, the standard shortcut is the Doomsday rule. Every year has a set of dates that all fall on the same weekday — that weekday is called the year's "doomsday". Once you know it, you can count to any other date in that year.

  1. Find the century anchor. For the 1900s the anchor is Wednesday, and for the 2000s it is Tuesday. These repeat every 400 years.
  2. Work out the year's doomsday. Take the last two digits of the year. Divide by 12 and keep the whole number, then add the remainder, then add the remainder divided by 4 (whole number again). Add that total to the century anchor, counting forward through the days of the week.
  3. Use a known doomsday date near your birthday. 4/4, 6/6, 8/8, 10/10 and 12/12 all land on the year's doomsday, as do 9/5, 5/9, 7/11 and 11/7. For January it is the 3rd in normal years and the 4th in leap years; for February it is the 28th, or the 29th in a leap year.
  4. Count forward or back in sevens. Move from the nearest doomsday date to your birthday, subtracting whole weeks as you go.

Worked example — 15 June 1985. The century anchor for the 1900s is Wednesday. For 85: 85 ÷ 12 = 7 remainder 1, and 1 ÷ 4 = 0. So 7 + 1 + 0 = 8. Counting eight days forward from Wednesday gives Thursday, so 1985's doomsday is Thursday. That means 6/6 was a Thursday. From 6 June to 15 June is nine days — one full week plus two — so 15 June 1985 was a Saturday.

The arithmetic is easy to get wrong on leap years and century boundaries, which is exactly what the calculator above removes. Use the manual method to sanity-check a single date; use the calculator when you want the answer plus the years ahead.

What day does my birthday fall on this year and next?

The weekday you were born on is fixed, but the weekday your birthday falls on changes every year. A common year is 365 days, which is 52 weeks plus one day, so your birthday normally moves forward one weekday each year. A leap year adds a second day, so the birthday jumps forward two weekdays whenever a 29 February sits between the two dates.

That produces a repeating pattern rather than a random one. Across most of a lifetime a birthday cycles through all seven weekdays roughly every 5 to 6 years, and the full pattern repeats every 28 years. What your birth weekday means

The weekday you were born on is a fixed historical fact, but folklore has long attached a character to each day. The best-known version is the English nursery rhyme "Monday's Child", first written down in the 1830s, which gives every day of the week its own line. Once the calculator tells you which day you were born on, this is the line people mean when they ask what it means to be born on a Monday, a Wednesday, or a Sunday.

Monday's child is fair of face,
Tuesday's child is full of grace,
Wednesday's child is full of woe,
Thursday's child has far to go,
Friday's child is loving and giving,
Saturday's child works hard for a living,
But the child that is born on the Sabbath day
Is bonny and blithe, and good and gay.

"Gay" here is the older sense of the word, meaning cheerful and lively; the "Sabbath day" is Sunday. The rhyme is a keepsake, not a horoscope — there is no evidence a birth weekday shapes personality — but it is a nice thing to know alongside the date.

Older and regional versions swap some of the lines around — a few give Thursday "fair of face" and Sunday's child "far to go" — so if you grew up with a different wording, both can be right. The version above is the one most widely printed today.
