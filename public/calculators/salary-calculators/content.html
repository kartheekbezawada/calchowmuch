Four focused tools for working out what you actually earn: your take-home pay after tax (with your pay converted between annual, monthly, weekly and hourly), the value of overtime, the real size of a raise, and what a bonus adds to your total compensation. Every figure is gross unless the tool says otherwise, and nothing you enter is stored.

Which salary calculator do you need?

If you want the number that reaches your bank account — after income tax, National Insurance or FICA, pension and student loan — use the Salary Calculator, which covers the UK, the US and Canada and produces a full pay-date sheet.

If you just need the same pay expressed a different way — an hourly rate as an annual salary, a yearly figure as a monthly one, a weekly wage as anything else — the Salary Calculator does that too: its Gross Pay mode converts between every pay period (annual, monthly, 4-weekly, biweekly, weekly, daily and hourly) from any starting point, before switching to a country for the after-tax figure.

For pay that varies, pick the tool that matches the question: the Overtime Pay Calculator for extra hours, the Raise Calculator for a permanent increase (and whether it beats inflation), and the Bonus Calculator for a one-off or percentage bonus on top of salary.

A note on pay periods, because it trips people up: monthly pay is your annual salary divided by 12, but a fortnightly (biweekly) paycheck is annual divided by 26, and a four-weekly paycheck is annual divided by 13 — not the same as monthly. And "gross" means before any deductions; your take-home is always lower, by an amount that depends on where you live.
