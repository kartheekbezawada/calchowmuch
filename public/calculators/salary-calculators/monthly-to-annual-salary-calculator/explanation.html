Income planning guide

Turn a monthly salary into an annual figure, gross or take-home

Start with the amount you're paid each month and get a clean yearly number back. Gross Pay mode just multiplies by 12 with no tax applied. Switch to UK take-home or USA take-home and the same monthly amount runs through the full tax calculation instead, so the annual figure you see is what actually lands in your bank account across the year, not the headline salary.

This matters most when an offer, payslip or freelance retainer only states a monthly number. A yearly figure is what you actually compare against other offers, mortgage affordability calculations and savings targets.

How to Guide

What This Calculator Does

Enter a monthly amount once. Gross Pay mode multiplies it by 12 for an annual figure, plus biweekly and weekly views. UK and USA modes run that same monthly amount through the relevant country's tax rules and show annual take-home pay instead, alongside a full breakdown of every deduction.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Choose Gross Pay, UK take-home, or USA take-home at the top.
  2. Enter your monthly salary.
  3. In UK mode, pick the country you pay tax in. In USA mode, confirm your state and filing status.
  4. Add a pension, student loan, 401(k), or bonus only if they apply to you.
  5. Read the annual figure, then check the biweekly and weekly views below it.

Gross pay conversion explained

How the conversion works

Annual pay is the monthly amount multiplied by 12. Biweekly divides the annual figure by 26, and weekly divides it by the weeks-per-year value in Work Schedule Assumptions, which defaults to 52. No tax, National Insurance, or FICA is applied in this mode.

When you need a country mode instead

Gross Pay mode is the right tool for comparing job offers stated as monthly salary, or for converting a monthly retainer into a yearly figure. If you want to know what actually reaches your account after tax, switch to UK take-home or USA take-home above.

UK deductions explained

Income Tax and the Personal Allowance

The calculator multiplies your monthly amount by 12 to get an annual gross figure, then applies your Personal Allowance (the amount you can earn tax-free) before charging Income Tax band by band on what remains. Earning over £100,000 a year starts to taper the allowance away, which is one reason the take-home figure can fall faster than the gross figure rises near that threshold.

National Insurance

National Insurance is worked out separately from Income Tax, on the same annual gross figure, using its own thresholds and rates. It is not reduced by pension contributions unless you use salary sacrifice.

Pension and student loan

Turn on the Pension or Student Loan panels if either applies. A net pay arrangement and salary sacrifice both reduce Income Tax; only salary sacrifice also reduces National Insurance, which makes it worth slightly more at the same contribution percentage.

US deductions explained

Federal tax and FICA

Your monthly amount is annualized, then the standard deduction is applied before federal income tax is charged bracket by bracket. FICA (Social Security and Medicare) is calculated separately on your full gross wages and is not reduced by 401(k) contributions.

State tax

State income tax depends entirely on which state you select. Nine states charge no income tax on wages at all; the rest use either a flat rate or their own graduated brackets, which is why the same monthly salary can produce noticeably different annual take-home pay in different states.

Filing status

Single, Married Filing Jointly, Married Filing Separately, and Head of Household each carry different brackets and standard deductions, so choosing the correct one changes the annual result by a meaningful amount at higher incomes.

Worked example: £4,500 a month in England

A monthly salary of £4,500.00 annualizes to £54,000.00 gross. In England, that leaves a Personal Allowance of £12,570, taxed at 20% up to £50,270 and 40% above it, plus National Insurance on the same gross figure. The result is an estimated annual take-home of roughly £40,900, or about £3,408 a month after deductions - noticeably less than the gross monthly figure most job adverts quote.

Worked example: $5,000 a month, Texas versus California

A monthly salary of $5,000.00 annualizes to $60,000.00 gross, filing Single. In Texas, which charges no state income tax, the estimated annual take-home is around $50,390. In California, state income tax brings that down to roughly $46,800 - over $3,500 less a year from the same monthly salary, purely because of where the work is based.

Common mistakes when converting monthly pay

  • Treating the Gross Pay annual figure as take-home pay when tax has not been applied.
  • Multiplying a take-home monthly payslip figure by 12 instead of entering the gross monthly amount.
  • Forgetting that a bonus paid in one month is taxed as part of that month's income, not spread evenly.
  • Ignoring the state selector in USA mode - it changes the annual result more than almost any other input.
  • Assuming doubling a monthly salary always doubles annual take-home, when tax bands mean it usually doesn't.

FAQ

Does this calculator show take-home pay or gross pay?

Both. Gross Pay mode converts one amount into every pay period with no tax applied. UK take-home mode estimates what actually reaches your bank account after Income Tax, National Insurance, pension and student loan.

How is UK take-home pay calculated here?

Your Personal Allowance is applied first, then Income Tax is charged band by band on what remains. National Insurance is calculated separately on gross earnings using its own thresholds, and student loan repayments are a percentage of income above your plan threshold.

Why does Scotland give a different result?

Scotland sets its own income tax bands and rates, which differ from England, Wales and Northern Ireland. The same salary produces a different take-home figure depending on where you are resident for tax purposes, which is why the region selector is not optional.

Does a pension contribution reduce my tax?

It depends on the relief method. A net pay arrangement reduces taxable income but not National Insurance. Salary sacrifice reduces both, so it is worth more at the same percentage. Relief at source reduces neither, and the scheme reclaims basic-rate relief on your behalf.

What is the difference between the effective rate and the marginal rate?

The effective rate is your total deductions as a percentage of gross pay. The marginal rate is the rate charged on your next pound earned. The marginal rate is almost always higher, and confusing the two is the most common misunderstanding about how tax works.

How is a bonus taxed?

The calculator adds the bonus to your annual income, works out total tax on the combined figure, and reports the difference. That is more accurate than applying your marginal rate to the bonus, because a bonus can push part of your income into a higher band.

Why is 4-weekly pay not the same as monthly pay?

Four-weekly pay means every 28 days, which is 13 payments a year. Monthly pay is 12 payments a year. The calculator divides annual pay by 13 for 4-weekly, never by taking a monthly figure and adjusting it.

Can I see the dates I will actually be paid?

Yes. Turn on Pay schedule and set your first pay date. The pay sheet lists your next 12 paydays with the gross, each deduction and the net amount landing on each one, including the payday your bonus falls on.

Why does my payslip differ from this estimate?

Payroll systems apply withholding rules that can differ from your annual tax position, and your employer may apply benefits, salary sacrifice arrangements or deductions not modelled here. This calculator estimates your annual position rather than replicating one payroll run.

Are my salary details stored anywhere?

No. All calculations run locally in your browser - no data is stored.

How is US take-home pay calculated here?

Your standard deduction is applied first, then federal income tax is charged bracket by bracket on what remains. FICA is calculated separately on your gross wages, and state income tax uses that state's own schedule and deductions.

Does my 401(k) reduce FICA as well as income tax?

No. A 401(k) reduces federal and state taxable income, but Social Security and Medicare are charged on your full gross wages regardless. This is the detail most paycheck calculators get wrong, and it makes the real saving smaller than the headline rate suggests.

Why does my state change the result so much?

Nine states levy no income tax on wages at all, around fifteen apply a single flat rate, and the rest use graduated brackets with their own deductions. On a $100,000 salary the difference between Texas and California is over $6,500 a year.

Are city or local income taxes included?

No. This calculator covers federal, FICA and state-level taxes only. If you live or work in a city with its own income tax. New York City, Philadelphia, Detroit and others. Your actual take-home will be lower than shown.

Which filing status should I choose?

Pick the status you file under: Single, Married Filing Jointly, Married Filing Separately, or Head of Household. Both the tax brackets and the standard deduction change with it, so on a $100,000 salary the federal tax alone can differ by several thousand dollars.
